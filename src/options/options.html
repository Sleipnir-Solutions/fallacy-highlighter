How to read the highlights

  • Relevance — support that feels relevant but doesn't bear on the claim (ad hominem, straw man, bandwagon…)
  • Presumption / weak induction — hidden assumptions or conclusions bigger than the evidence (false dilemma, cherry picking, slippery slope…)
  • Ambiguity — a word or claim doing double duty (equivocation, motte and bailey…)
  • Formal — invalid structure (affirming the consequent…)

Solid underline = medium or high confidence; dashed = low. Each highlight is a possible issue, not a verdict: the same pattern can be reasonable in one context and fallacious in another, and a flawed argument doesn't make its conclusion false. Automated detection is imperfect. In the MAFALDA benchmark (Helwe et al., NAACL 2024), human annotators scored far higher at spotting whether a passage is fallacious (F1 0.749) than at naming which fallacy it is (F1 0.186), which is why each highlight leads with the family.

Privacy: when Claude is used, the visible text of the page (or your selection), plus the page title and web address, is sent to Anthropic's API under your key. Nothing is sent until you start an analysis. Tip: use a key from its own Anthropic Console workspace with a monthly spend limit.
